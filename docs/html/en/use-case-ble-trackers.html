USE CASE INTRODUCTION

Find your Bluetooth tracker

You know your tracker is nearby, but you need help narrowing down where it is.

THE PROBLEM Find My can show the approximate location where a tracker was detected by a nearby Apple device, but it does not provide a precise direction to the tracker.

ObjectRadar was created to help turn this approximate information into a practical local search.

BEFORE YOU START

Understand what the indicators mean

A few simple points will help you understand the search results.

POSITION

The location shown by Find My is the location of the device that detected the tracker. It is not necessarily the tracker's exact location.

Approximate location

The detecting iPhone does not give an exact tag position.

Several detections

Multiple detections can narrow the search area, but do not prove an exact position.

Why can the displayed location be different?

A tracker can be detected by a nearby Apple device. The reported location therefore gives you a useful starting area rather than an exact position.

SIGNAL STRENGTH (RSSI)

RSSI indicates the relative strength of the Bluetooth signal. A stronger signal can help you determine whether you are moving in the right direction, but it cannot provide a reliable distance.

Move slowly

Move — wait — compare — repeat — take your time.

Why can the signal change unexpectedly?

Walls, furniture, vehicles and other objects can affect Bluetooth signals. Compare several measurements instead of relying on a single reading.

TIME INTERVAL (FRAMES COMMUNICATION)

The tracker may not transmit continuously. The interval between received advertising frames can vary.

What does the dot and the ring around the target mean?

The dot indicates the interval between received advertising frames.
Blue rings represent the search levels, while the two green levels indicate very close proximity. The ring color does not represent distance.

SEARCHING AMONG SEVERAL TAGS

If you own several tags, temporarily move the known tags away from the search area when possible. This reduces the number of nearby devices and makes it easier to identify the tag you are looking for.

NEARBY TAGS

ObjectRadar may detect a Find My-compatible tag that does not belong to you. A detected tag is not necessarily your tag.

COMPATIBILITY

ObjectRadar is designed to detect Bluetooth trackers compatible with the Find My protocol and visible to iOS.

Why might a tracker not be detected?

ObjectRadar cannot detect or display a device that is not compatible with the Find My protocol, is not authorized for use with it, or is filtered by iOS. ObjectRadar does not bypass Apple's device compatibility or access restrictions.

ASSOCIATION

Search for an object, not a number

ObjectRadar associates a detected tracker with an object you know.

OBJECTS ASSOCIATION WITH DEVICES

Instead of searching for an unknown Bluetooth device, you can search for an object such as your keys, bag or other personal item.

Why is this useful?

A detected tracker is initially associated with the object you believe it belongs to. If the association is wrong, you can correct it at any time — before continuing the search or after discovering that you found the wrong item.

ROTATING IDENTIFIERS

Some trackers periodically change the Bluetooth identifier used for advertising.

Why can a tracker temporarily disappear from the scan?

A tracker may change its advertising identifier during a long search. Restarting the scan does not require you to associate the object again. When the same device UUID is detected again, its existing object association is retained.
