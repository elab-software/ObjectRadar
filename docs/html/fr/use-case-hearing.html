BEFORE YOU START

Check the Bluetooth connection

A few simple points will help you understand the search results.

PAIRING

Your hearing aid should already be associated with the iPhone you are using for the search. A hearing aid paired with another device may not be available to ObjectRadar.

Searching for already paired hearing aids

To see your hearing aids in ObjectRadar, they must first be paired with your iPhone

Why does the paired iPhone matter?

The iPhone is the device that communicates with and discovers the hearing aid. This is different from a Bluetooth tracker that can be detected by nearby devices.

BLUETOOTH

ObjectRadar uses the Bluetooth information available to iOS to identify and search for the hearing aid.

How does the signal help me find it?

As with Bluetooth trackers, the RSSI value can help you determine whether the signal is getting stronger or weaker as you move. It does not provide a reliable distance.

ASSOCIATION

Search for an object, not a number

ObjectRadar associates a detected hearing with an object you know.

OBJECTS ASSOCIATION WITH DEVICES

You can associate each hearing aid (left and right) with a personnal object with a custum label

Why is this useful?

A detected hearing aid is not automatically associated with an object. You can associate it with the object you believe it belongs to. If the association is incorrect, you can assign the object to another hearing at any time — before continuing the search or after discovering that you found the wrong item. ObjectRadar automatically updates the association.

ROTATING IDENTIFIERS

A paired hearing aid may keep the same Bluetooth UUID for a long period, but iOS may assign a different UUID in some situations.

Why can a hearing aid temporarily disappear from the scan?

If the UUID changes, ObjectRadar may detect the hearing aid as a new device. In this case, the object association may need to be created again manually. When the same device UUID is detected again, its existing object association is retained.
